How Costs Are Shared on a Dividing Fence

A fence built on the actual boundary line is a dividing fence, and Queensland's dividing fence framework is the basis for working out contribution. Both owners are generally responsible equally, but only up to a sufficient dividing fence. That means roughly 0.5 m to 1.8 m high and built from prescribed materials such as palings, chain wire, metal panels or brick.

The trap is the upgrade. If one owner wants a taller run, a premium finish or Colorbond instead of a cheaper sufficient fence, that extra cost sits with the owner asking for it. A neighbour can still agree to more, but the quote should show the sufficient fence and the upgrade as separate lines so the money talk stays clear.

Where agreement is not reached, a notice to contribute can set out the land, the fence line, the work scope, the estimated cost and a written quote. If there is no agreement within a month, either owner can take the matter to QCAT within the following two months. What a Useful Written Quote Should Show

A written quote earns its keep when it can be handed to the person next door without a phone call to explain it. For a shared line, the useful habit is to ask for the scope in layers: material, height, run length, removal of the old fence, gates, access limits and any automation, each visible as its own line. Then ask how the quote can be split so each owner understands their share of the sufficient fence and any upgrade they have chosen.

  • Ask whether the quote separates removal, new posts, panels and gates rather than bundling them into one figure.
  • Ask what happens where access is tight, the line changes grade or a gate must hang from the same structure.
  • Keep the written scope beside the itemised price so both households work from the same document.

Height, Corners and Pool Barriers

Height is one of the few hard numbers in the local rules. A fence at or under 2 m above natural ground is treated as accepted development; crossing 2 m requires certifier sign-off. A fence on top of a retaining wall counts the wall in that measurement, so an ordinary fence can become a higher combined structure without anyone meaning it to.

Corner sites carry their own check, because intersections come with sight-line rules. The question is whether front fencing lets drivers see who is coming, not whether a taller screen would look better on the front boundary.

Pool fencing is narrower again. Glass and aluminium pool barriers are built to the Queensland pool safety standard, and only a licensed pool safety inspector can sign off a pool barrier. Treat that as its own compliance pathway, separate from a side or rear boundary fence.

Material Choice Follows the Block

Materials make more sense when chosen for the block rather than the brochure. Colorbond steel is described as the workhorse boundary option, timber palings suit established streets, and lapped and capped timber is the step up when gaps are not wanted. PVC removes painting from the picture, while glass and aluminium appear first as pool compliance materials.

Rural work follows a different path. Out toward acreage country, strained wire, ringlock, star pickets, driveway gates and automation belong to the same site conversation, with posts, terrain, vehicle access and gate loads doing the real deciding. Use the material list as a shortlisting tool, then let the quote answer the block. If the old fence is leaning, the line changes grade or a gate hangs from the same structure, the cheapest panel is rarely the job that is easiest to own.

  1. Map the run. Mark the fence line, gates, pool areas, retaining walls and access limits before comparing materials.
  2. Talk to the neighbour early. Confirm whether the line is shared and agree on a sufficient fence before quotes are treated as final.
  3. Get written itemised quotes. Make sure each quote shows material, height, length, removal, gates and automation as separate lines, with upgrades apart from the sufficient fence.
  4. Check the rules that apply. Confirm the 2 m height point, how a retaining wall affects measurement, sight-line limits on corners and the pool barrier pathway.
